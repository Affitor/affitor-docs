This page lays out what each one is, what it costs with the numbers checked in October 2026, and which one fits your stage, plus the option that charges nothing until your program works.

## Quick answer: PartnerStack or Rewardful?

Choose by scope, not by feature list. If you need affiliate and referral tracking for a Stripe SaaS, Rewardful is the correct size: $49/month, set up with two scripts or a one-click integration, REST API on every plan. If you run a multi-type partner program (affiliates plus resellers plus referral partners) at mid-market or enterprise scale, PartnerStack's PRM suite and partner marketplace are built for exactly that, and its Enterprise plan adds a designated customer success manager. The trap is buying PartnerStack's scope before you have the partner motion to fill it. And if you are early enough that even $49/month of fixed cost feels premature, Affitor charges $0 until a program has made $10,000 through affiliates.

## The real difference: PRM suite vs affiliate app

The pricing gap is not a discount or a markup: it reflects two different products.

**PartnerStack is a partner-relationship management platform.** Affiliate tracking is one feature inside it: on Launch you pick affiliate link tracking or lead and deal registration, and Enterprise runs both. The rest is the reason it costs what it does: marketplace access pitched as "Get discovered by active B2B partners", partner payments, onboarding and training tools, CRM integrations, and on Growth a learning management system, partner challenges, and market-development-fund (MDF) management. You buy PartnerStack when "affiliates" is one of several partner types you manage, and when partner sourcing itself is a channel you invest in.

**Rewardful is a focused affiliate app.** It tracks affiliate and referral conversions for Stripe (and Paddle Classic), lets you pay commissions by PayPal or Wise bulk payouts or through its optional Managed Payouts, and gives each affiliate a dashboard, and it stops there, deliberately. That focus is why it sets up in minutes and costs $49 to start. You buy Rewardful when you want an affiliate program running this week without a sales call.

Neither is "better." They answer different questions. The mistake is letting a comparison article flatten them onto one axis.

## Pricing, checked October 2026

**Rewardful** is straightforward and self-serve ([rewardful.com/pricing](https://www.rewardful.com/pricing)):

- **Starter, $49/mo:** up to $7,500/mo in affiliate-attributed revenue, 1 campaign, up to 2 team members
- **Growth, $99/mo:** up to $15,000/mo, unlimited campaigns and team, branded portal, custom domain
- **Enterprise, $149+/mo:** up to $30,000/mo on the entry Enterprise tier, phone support, one-click PayPal payouts
- A 0% transaction fee on every plan (optional Managed Payouts cost 3% of payouts), a 14-day trial, 12 months for the price of 10 on annual billing, and its REST API included even on the $49 plan.

**PartnerStack** publishes plans but no prices ([partnerstack.com/pricing](https://www.partnerstack.com/pricing)):

- **Launch:** affiliate link tracking or lead and deal registration (pick one), marketplace access, an AI and MCP connector, partner payments, onboarding and training tools, CRM integrations
- **Growth:** adds advanced integrations, a learning management system, partner challenges and incentives, MDF management, advanced partner segmentation
- **Enterprise:** full access to link tracking and lead and deal registration, custom workflows, a designated CSM

Every plan starts with a demo: "Your final plan and pricing are confirmed during your demo." The page states no limit on partners or internal users, and its FAQ says most teams are up and running "in weeks." (Third-party reports of a per-commission cut circulate, but PartnerStack does not state one on its pages, so we do not assert it.)

The honest read: these two are not competing for the same buyer, and the pricing process says so. One publishes a $49 plan you can start today; the other prices your program in a demo.

## Attribution

**Rewardful** uses cookie-based attribution with a [60-day default window](https://help.rewardful.com/en/articles/2155812-how-long-are-referral-cookies-valid) you can change per campaign, and lets you choose first-touch or last-touch credit. It is simple and well understood, and it shares the cookie cohort's blind spot: when a buyer clears cookies, blocks them, or switches devices before converting, the affiliate can silently lose credit.

**PartnerStack's** Launch plan offers affiliate link tracking or lead and deal registration, and for Stripe its docs describe a webhook that listens for charge and subscription events. Beyond that we will not describe its attribution model: for an enterprise PRM it is usually part of the implementation conversation with their team rather than a published spec.

If attribution durability is a deciding factor, that is worth a direct question to each vendor, and it is the specific gap Affitor is built around, below.
