SaaS teams leave Rewardful for three reasons: revenue caps that force plan upgrades as you grow, cookie-based attribution that drops conversions when the cookie is lost, and a narrower feature set than some rivals. If none of those hurt yet, keep Rewardful. Setup is two scripts or a one-click Stripe integration, the transaction fee is 0%, and the REST API is included on every plan, including the $49 one. Plenty of programs never need more.

This guide compares five alternatives worth shortlisting in 2026: Affitor, FirstPromoter, Tolt, Dub Partners, and PartnerStack. One disclosure before we start: we build Affitor. It is one of the five tools below, and this post tells you plainly where the others beat it.

## Why teams outgrow Rewardful

Rewardful's model is a flat subscription with revenue-capped plans. Per [rewardful.com/pricing](https://www.rewardful.com/pricing), checked October 2026: Starter is $49/mo for up to $7,500/mo in affiliate-attributed revenue, with 1 campaign and up to 2 team members. Growth is $99/mo for up to $15,000/mo, with unlimited campaigns, a branded affiliate portal, and a custom domain. Enterprise starts at $149/mo and covers up to $30,000/mo on its entry tier. There is no $0 plan; every plan has a 14-day trial. The 0% transaction fee is genuine: the subscription is what you pay Rewardful, and its optional Managed Payouts cost 3% of payouts.

The caps are the first thing that bites. A program doing well crosses $7,500/mo in affiliate revenue and the tool's price doubles, not because you used more software but because your affiliates performed. Cross $15,000/mo and it steps again.

The second issue is attribution. Rewardful tracks with cookies, with first- or last-touch credit and a [60-day default window](https://help.rewardful.com/en/articles/2155812-how-long-are-referral-cookies-valid) you can change per campaign. When the cookie is gone (cleared, blocked, expired, or the buyer switches devices) before the customer converts, the referral is gone with it, and the affiliate who earned the sale does not get paid.

The third is the feature set. Rewardful covers the core well: coupon code tracking, double-sided incentives, and self-referral fraud detection are on every plan. But its plan table lists no multi-tier commissions, which FirstPromoter lists on every plan, and its billing connections are Stripe and Paddle Classic, not Paddle Billing.

## What to evaluate in a replacement

Three questions separate the five tools below faster than any feature checklist.

**How does it charge?** Flat subscription, percentage of results, or both. Watch for double tolls: some tools charge a subscription and then add a fee on payouts or transactions on top.

**What happens when the cookie dies?** Every tool here starts from a partner click. The difference is what carries the referral afterwards: a browser cookie all the way to each sale, or a customer record that the signup ties to the partner, which later payments match against.

**Can your coding agent do the integration?** In 2026 a lot of Stripe SaaS integration work is done by AI agents. By October 2026 most tools here have an MCP server of some kind: Dub on Business, a Tolt connection to Claude, FirstPromoter in beta, PartnerStack on Launch, and Affitor in beta, while Rewardful's MCP server covers its developer docs. The question to ask is whether an agent can install tracking and then prove it works with a test sale.

## 1. Affitor: best for paying on results

![Affitor's Partners view: active partners and applications in one table with live stats](/blog/shots/partners-table-live.png)

Affitor is our product, so here is the model stated plainly: you pay nothing until your affiliate program pays you.

### Key features

Tracking starts like the rest: a partner click sets a first-party cookie, and the partner whose click creates the customer keeps that customer. What carries the referral after that is the signup: you send your own customer key, it rides Stripe Checkout and subscription metadata as `affitor_customer_key`, and later payments are matched to the customer rather than to the cookie. The attribution window is set per program, 60 days by default, and is checked once, at the first attributed payment: the click must be no older than the window at signup (or at that payment, if there was no signup). Renewals of an attributed customer are not re-checked. The chain is documented in [how Affitor tracking fits together](/docs/brand/tracking/tracking-overview).

The agent surface is live today: a `skill.md` runbook an agent can follow end to end, the `affitor` CLI, browser and server SDKs plus an MCP server (`@affitor/sdk` and `@affitor/mcp`, both labeled beta), and `npx affitor onboard`, which installs tracking and then fires a test click, lead, and sale through your live integration until it reports `integration_verified`. You (or your agent) get proof the integration works before a real affiliate joins. The full setup path is walked in [How to create a Stripe affiliate program](/blog/stripe-affiliate-program).

![Affitor advertiser dashboard: performance overview tracking revenue, partners, clicks, signups, and paid customers for one program](/docs/brand/dashboard.png)

Every live program is listed on the [Affitor marketplace](https://affitor.com/marketplace), and the Affitor team helps you recruit, onboard, and train partners ([affitor.com/join](https://affitor.com/join)). Day-to-day operations stay deliberately small: one partner table holds active partners and applications, and you approve each application by hand or set auto-accept.

### Pricing

$0/mo, no setup fee, and nothing per partner or per seat. You pay $0 on the first $10,000 of affiliate revenue per program, then 3.5% of the sales affiliates drove (checked Oct 2026, [affitor.com/pricing](https://affitor.com/pricing)). If your affiliates generate nothing, you pay nothing. There are no tiers and no revenue caps.

### Pros & cons

**Pros:** no subscription and no caps, so cost follows results; renewals stay attributed after the cookie is gone; a marketplace listing with recruiting help; an integration an agent can complete and test with a synthetic sale.

**Cons:** a percentage fee grows with your affiliate revenue, while a flat subscription stays flat until its cap, so price your own expected volume on both pricing pages before you choose. Affitor reads Stripe natively; on another billing system you wire it up through its API or SDK, while FirstPromoter connects five billing systems natively. Partners withdraw by bank transfer or Wise, where Rewardful offers PayPal mass payouts and Wise bulk payouts on every plan.

**Rewardful:** $49 to $149+/mo from day one, 0% transaction fee, cookie attribution.
**Affitor:** $0/mo, 3.5% of affiliate-driven sales after the first $10,000 per program, a click cookie and then the signup customer key.

## 2. FirstPromoter: best for billing providers beyond Stripe

FirstPromoter is the practical answer when your billing stack rules Rewardful out, and it lists commission and tax features Rewardful's plan table does not.

### Key features

Native connections to five billing systems (Stripe, Paddle, Chargebee, Recurly, Braintree), recurring and lifetime commissions, multi-tier commissions up to 3 levels, W-9 and W-8BEN collection in the affiliate portal, fraud detection, and APIs with webhooks, all on every plan. Its MCP server is in beta. A custom dashboard domain arrives on the Business plan.

### Pricing

Per [firstpromoter.com/pricing](https://firstpromoter.com/pricing), checked October 2026: Starter is $49/mo for up to $5,000/mo in affiliate revenue, 3 campaigns, and up to 1,000 affiliates. Business is $99/mo for up to $15,000/mo with unlimited campaigns and affiliates and 2 websites. Enterprise starts at $149/mo above that. Team members are unlimited on every plan. 14-day trial, no credit card required. No transaction fee is listed on the pricing page.

### Pros & cons

**Pros:** five native billing connections; multi-tier and recurring commission rules; tax-form collection and fraud detection on every plan; 3 campaigns on the entry plan where Rewardful allows 1 (FirstPromoter's campaigns are promoter groups with their own commissions).

**Cons:** the $5,000/mo cap on Starter is the lowest of the three flat-fee trackers here, so upgrade pressure arrives earliest. A second website needs Business, one-click PayPal payouts need Business, and managed payouts (FirstPromoter pays partners for you) need [Enterprise 2 and up, for SaaS companies, subject to approval](https://help.firstpromoter.com/en/articles/9998853-how-managed-auto-payouts-work). Tracking is a cookie with first- or last-click credit and a cookie life you set. For the full head-to-head with Rewardful, see [Rewardful vs FirstPromoter](/blog/rewardful-vs-firstpromoter).

**FirstPromoter:** API and webhooks on every plan, from $49/mo.
**Affitor:** API, CLI, and a beta MCP server at $0/mo on every program.

## 3. Tolt: best for unlimited affiliates on a flat fee

Tolt is a clean modern product in the Rewardful mold, and it removes the affiliate-count question entirely.

### Key features

Unlimited affiliates and referrals on every plan. Payout rails are the broadest here: PayPal, Wise, local bank transfer, crypto, and wire, with auto payouts from the Growth plan up. At $99/mo Tolt gives you more revenue headroom than Rewardful's $99 plan ($20,000 vs $15,000 cap). Tolt also has a [REST API](https://docs.tolt.com/introduction) and an MCP connection to Claude.

### Pricing

Per [tolt.com/pricing](https://tolt.com/pricing), checked October 2026: Basic is $69/mo for up to $10,000/mo in affiliate revenue with 2 programs and manual payouts. Growth is $99/mo for up to $20,000/mo with 5 programs, auto payouts, and W-9/W-8 collection with 1099 filing. Pro is $199/mo for up to $50,000/mo with unlimited programs. 14-day trial, no credit card required, 30-day refund policy.

### Pros & cons

**Pros:** unlimited affiliates everywhere, wide payout rails, and the most revenue headroom of the flat-fee trio at $99.

**Cons:** the nuance sits in the payout fees. Tolt markets "0% commission", and that claim has a footnote: auto payouts carry a 2% processing fee, and the Basic plan avoids the fee because its payouts are manual. Tax-form collection starts on Growth. Tracking starts from a cookie: its JavaScript stores the partner click with a cookie duration, and a signup call ties that click to your customer.

**Tolt:** $69 to $199/mo, plus 2% on auto payouts.
**Affitor:** $0/mo, one 3.5% fee on affiliate-driven sales after the first $10,000 per program.

## 4. Dub Partners: best for developer-first teams

Dub is affiliate infrastructure shaped like developer tooling.

### Key features

Dub's pricing page lists a REST API, an MCP server, native SDKs, and real-time event webhooks on every Partners plan, plus tax compliance handled for you, so you do not collect W-9, W-8 or 1099 forms yourself. If you already run Dub for link infrastructure, adding Partners keeps links, analytics, and payouts in one platform.

### Pricing

Per [dub.co/pricing](https://dub.co/pricing), checked October 2026: Partners needs a paid plan. Business is $90/mo with partner payouts up to $2,500/mo and up to 500 partners. Advanced is $300/mo with payouts up to $15,000/mo and unlimited partners. Enterprise is custom, billed annually, with unlimited payouts and a marketplace listing. The [payout fee](https://dub.co/help/article/partner-payouts) is 5% if you fund payouts by ACH, 8% by card (3% and 6% on Enterprise).

### Pros & cons

**Pros:** API, MCP server, SDKs, and webhooks on the entry plan; tax compliance handled; one platform for links, analytics, and payouts.

**Cons:** the toll structure. You pay the subscription and a payout fee of 5% if you fund payouts by ACH, 8% by card (3% and 6% on Enterprise), and the payout caps meter your program's growth by plan: a Business-plan program cannot pay partners more than $2,500 in a month. The marketplace listing and partner network are Enterprise features.

**Dub Partners:** $90/mo plus a payout fee of 5% if you fund payouts by ACH or 8% by card, capped by plan.
**Affitor:** $0/mo plus 3.5% of affiliate-driven sales after the first $10,000 per program, no revenue cap.

## 5. PartnerStack: best for enterprise partner programs

PartnerStack is not really a Rewardful substitute; it is a different category, priced like one.

### Key features

A full partner-relationship-management suite ([pricing page](https://www.partnerstack.com/pricing), checked Oct 2026): PartnerStack Marketplace access on every plan, affiliate link tracking or lead and deal registration on Launch (both on Enterprise), partner payments, an MCP connector, and on Growth a learning management system, partner challenges, and MDF management. If you run affiliates, resellers, and referral partners as one program at scale, it is the serious option on this page.

### Pricing

PartnerStack does not publish prices. Per [partnerstack.com/pricing](https://www.partnerstack.com/pricing), checked October 2026, Launch, Growth, and Enterprise all start with a demo, and "Your final plan and pricing are confirmed during your demo." Pricing depends on program size, feature needs, and level of support. Its FAQ says there is no limit on partners or internal users.

### Pros & cons

**Pros:** marketplace distribution, multi-type partner motions, and enterprise operations no point tool on this page attempts.

**Cons:** the process. You see a price after a demo, and its FAQ says most teams are up and running in weeks. For Stripe, its docs describe a webhook that listens for charge and subscription events; we make no other claims about its attribution. For an early or mid-stage SaaS replacing a $49 tool, this is the wrong aisle; for a partnerships team that has outgrown a pure affiliate motion, it is the right one. If PartnerStack's process is the reason you are here, the dedicated [PartnerStack alternatives guide](/blog/partnerstack-alternatives) goes deeper.

**PartnerStack:** priced in a demo, full PRM suite.
**Affitor:** self-serve signup, $0/mo, affiliate programs without the PRM layer.

## Which one should you pick?

The honest segmentation is by stage, because the pricing models behave differently as affiliate revenue grows.

**$0–500K ARR: pick Affitor, or Rewardful if you want a known flat cost.** At this stage your affiliate program earns little or nothing yet, and a $49–$90 subscription is pure downside risk. Affitor costs $0 until a program has made $10,000 through affiliates, so the software decision needs no budget. If you would rather pay a predictable $49/mo for a familiar tool, Rewardful is the default.

**$500K–5M ARR: compare at your own volume.** If affiliate-driven revenue is steady, a capped flat plan is predictable: Rewardful Growth at $99/mo covers up to $15,000/mo, and Tolt's $99 plan covers $20,000/mo with unlimited affiliates. If you bill through Chargebee, Recurly, or Braintree, FirstPromoter's native connections decide it. If affiliate revenue is still lumpy, Affitor's pay-on-results model keeps quiet months at $0.

**$5M+ ARR: think in programs, not trackers.** If you run affiliates plus resellers plus referral partners with a partner manager, PartnerStack's PRM suite is the real option. If it is still a pure affiliate motion, the Enterprise plans of Rewardful ($149+/mo) or FirstPromoter (from $149/mo), or Dub's custom Enterprise with its lower payout fees (3% by ACH, 6% by card), cover the volume.

## FAQ

### What is the best Rewardful alternative for SaaS?

It depends on why you are leaving. For SaaS that wants no monthly fee, Affitor is the best fit: $0 until a program has made $10,000 through affiliates, then 3.5% of affiliate-driven sales. FirstPromoter is the strongest pick when you bill outside Stripe, and Tolt when you want unlimited affiliates and more revenue headroom on a flat subscription.

### How does Affitor's pricing compare with Rewardful's?

They charge in different shapes. Affitor has no subscription: $0 on the first $10,000 of affiliate revenue per program, then 3.5% of affiliate-driven sales. Rewardful charges a monthly plan by affiliate-revenue tier ($49, $99 or $149+) with a 0% transaction fee (checked October 2026). A new program pays nothing on Affitor until it has made $10,000 through affiliates; after that, compare both at your own monthly volume.

### Does Rewardful charge transaction fees?

No. Rewardful states a 0% transaction fee on affiliate-attributed revenue on every plan ($49, $99 or $149+ a month, checked October 2026). Optional Managed Payouts cost 3% of payouts, and payment-provider fees are separate. The trade-off is revenue-capped plans: $7,500 a month in affiliate revenue on Starter, $15,000 on Growth, and $30,000 on the entry Enterprise tier.

### Which affiliate software has no monthly fee?

In this comparison, Affitor: $0 a month, $0 on the first $10,000 of affiliate revenue per program, then 3.5% of affiliate-driven sales. The others charge a monthly plan: Rewardful $49, FirstPromoter $49, Tolt $69, Dub Partners $90, and PartnerStack prices every plan in a demo. Outside this comparison, PromoteKit has a $0 plan for up to 3 referrals and Endorsely a $0 plan below $1,000 a month in affiliate revenue (checked October 2026).

### How much does affiliate tracking software cost in 2026?

Entry prices checked October 2026: Rewardful and FirstPromoter $49/month, Tolt $69/month, Dub Partners $90/month plus a payout fee of 5% if you fund payouts by ACH or 8% by card, PartnerStack priced during a demo, and Affitor $0/month with 3.5% of affiliate-driven sales after the first $10,000 per program. ### Why do SaaS teams leave Rewardful?

Three common reasons: revenue-capped plans that force upgrades as affiliate sales grow, cookie-based attribution (60-day default window) that loses the referral when the cookie is gone before the customer converts, and a narrower feature set than FirstPromoter, whose plans list multi-tier commissions and five native billing connections.
