## What happened, and why

Lemon Squeezy was a popular way for indie founders and small software companies to sell online. It acted as a "merchant of record", which means it handled payments, sales tax, and invoices on your behalf. Many stores also used its built-in affiliate program to let creators and partners earn a commission for every customer they sent.

Stripe [acquired Lemon Squeezy in 2024](https://www.lemonsqueezy.com/blog/stripe-acquires-lemon-squeezy). Since then, the same team built a new product called Managed Payments, directly on Stripe. In his goodbye post, cofounder JR Farr explains that running two platforms meant duplicate work across engineering, security, and compliance. The company chose to focus on one product.

Here are the dates that matter, taken from the official migration guide:

| Date | What happens |
|---|---|
| November 16, 2026 | Lemon Squeezy stops accepting new users and new stores |
| December 2, 2026 | Migration tooling for Stripe Managed Payments launches for eligible stores |
| Mid-January 2027 | Recommended latest date to start your migration |
| March 31, 2027 | Lemon Squeezy stops processing transactions, including subscription renewals. Lemon Squeezy Affiliates closes |


Lemon Squeezy says the full move can take up to 8 weeks. That is why mid-January is the real deadline for starting, not March 31.

### The part most merchants miss

The migration guide lists features that do not come across to Managed Payments. Affiliates is one of them. In Lemon Squeezy's own words, Managed Payments "doesn't include built-in affiliate features for merchants or an affiliate community", and you will "need a separate affiliate platform".

Two more points from the same guide are worth reading twice:

* Affiliate links are on the guide's list of links that will break, and the guide is clear: "None of these redirect automatically." Every link your partners have posted in videos, newsletters, and blog posts will stop earning on March 31, 2027.
* Lemon Squeezy is telling affiliates to "stop promoting" your products and remove or update their links by that date. Your partners are hearing this message right now. If you do not tell them where your program is going, many will simply stop.

So the question is not only "where does my checkout go?" It is also "how do I keep my partners selling?"

## Which situation are you in?

You can be in more than one. For example, you may sell through Lemon Squeezy and also run an affiliate program for that product.

### 1. You run a Lemon Squeezy affiliate program

**What happens to your program**

* Lemon Squeezy Affiliates closes on March 31, 2027. Referrals after that date are not tracked or paid.
* Your affiliate links on Lemon Squeezy do not redirect anywhere.
* If you move your checkout to Stripe Managed Payments, you get no affiliate features there. You need a separate affiliate platform.

**What to do now**

* **Save your affiliate list.** Write down each affiliate's name, email, commission rate and what you still owe them. As of November 25, 2026, Lemon Squeezy has not published a way to export your affiliates or commission history. Save it now so you do not depend on a tool that may or may not come later.

  * Does it charge a monthly fee before your program makes a sale?
  * Does it add a fee every time you pay your partners?
  * Does it have a marketplace where new partners can find you, or only on its top plan?
  * Does it work with your new checkout, even if that checkout is not Stripe?
* **Tell your affiliates,** as Lemon Squeezy asks you to: when your Lemon Squeezy program ends, when to stop using old links, where the program is moving, where to get new links, how outstanding commissions will be paid, and who to contact with questions. ### 2. You take payments through Lemon Squeezy: the alternatives

**The official path: Stripe Managed Payments**

* Pricing is 3.5% per transaction plus Stripe's processing fees. Stores that migrate from Lemon Squeezy get a promotional rate of 1.5% through December 1, 2027.
* It is open only to businesses in 35 locations: the US, Canada, 29 European countries, Australia, Hong Kong, Japan and Singapore. Many other countries are not supported. For sellers outside those locations, Lemon Squeezy suggests forming a US company through Stripe Atlas.
* It covers eligible digital products only, such as SaaS, software, e-books, digital downloads and self-paced courses.

**If you cannot move to Stripe**

Many Lemon Squeezy sellers are in this position: individuals without a registered company, or sellers in countries Stripe does not support. ### 3. You earn commissions as a Lemon Squeezy affiliate

**What is confirmed**

* Lemon Squeezy asks affiliates to stop promoting and to remove or update their referral links by March 31, 2027. Referrals after that date are not tracked or paid.
* The Lemon Squeezy affiliate marketplace closes with the program.
* Until then, payouts work as before: commissions are held for 30 days, payouts are made on the 14th and 28th of each month by bank transfer or PayPal, each store sets its own minimum payout (the default is $10), and a 2% fee applies to each referral.

**Not announced yet (as of November 25, 2026)**

* Whether commissions still inside the 30-day hold when the program closes will be paid.
* What happens to approved commissions that are below a store's minimum payout. Are they paid or cancelled?
* The date of the final affiliate payout.
* If a store moves to another affiliate platform, whether your past referrals still earn commissions there.

Lemon Squeezy tells affiliates to contact each store they promote to find out whether the program will continue elsewhere, when updated referral links will be ready, and how outstanding commissions or payouts will be handled. If you promote several stores, you have to ask each one.

**Questions to send each store**

1. Is your affiliate program moving to another platform? When will I get my new link?
2. Will commissions still on hold when Lemon Squeezy closes be paid, and when?
3. How will you pay approved commissions that are below the minimum payout?
4. If customers I referred renew after you move, will I still earn commissions?

If you have not received an email from Lemon Squeezy or from the stores you promote, do not wait. Send these questions to each store now.

**Where to find new programs**

The Lemon Squeezy affiliate marketplace closes on March 31, 2027. If you promote software and need new programs, the Affitor marketplace lists SaaS programs, each with its commission, how long it recurs and how long the referral window lasts.

## What you need in a replacement

You do not need to compare twenty tools to make a good choice. Start with what your Lemon Squeezy program did for you, and make sure the new platform does all of it.

**1. Tracking that works with your new checkout.**
If you are moving to Stripe, your affiliate tool should read sales from Stripe directly, including subscription renewals and refunds. If a customer refunds, the commission should reverse on its own. You should not have to rebuild this with custom code. If you move to another checkout such as Paddle, Polar or Dodo Payments, your affiliate tool needs to receive sales from it through an API.

**2. Partner payouts you do not run by hand.**
On Lemon Squeezy, paying affiliates happened inside the platform. Your replacement should calculate commissions, hold them until your refund window closes, and pay partners for you. Spreadsheets and manual bank transfers do not scale past a handful of partners.

**3. A clean way to move your existing affiliates.**
Your current partners are the most valuable part of your program. They already know your product. Look for a platform where they can join quickly, get a new link, and start promoting again the same week.

**4. A way to find new partners.**
Lemon Squeezy had an affiliate community. Managed Payments does not. If you only bring your existing partners, your program can only shrink. A marketplace where creators look for programs to promote helps you replace partners who drop off.

**5. Pricing that fits a small business.**
Many affiliate tools charge a monthly fee before your program has earned a single dollar. For a solo founder or an early startup, that is a cost with no guaranteed return.
